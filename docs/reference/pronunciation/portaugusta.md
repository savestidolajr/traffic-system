# Port Augusta (5AU 97.9) Pronunciation Guide

Pronunciation reference for the 5AU 97.9 traffic bulletin covering Port Augusta, the upper Spencer Gulf, and the southern Flinders Ranges. Alias respellings feed the ElevenLabs eleven_v3 pronunciation dictionary; each operator should audition every respelling in the ElevenLabs studio before taking it to air.

## How to read this
Alias respellings only (eleven_v3 ignores IPA); each replaces only the proper-noun token; audition before air.

## Suburbs & localities

| Name | Avoid (common / TTS mispronunciation) | Correct (plain phonetic, e.g. "muh-NASH") | Alias respelling | Notes & source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Port Augusta | "porr AW-gus-ta" or "port AW-gus-tuh" | "pawt aw-GUS-tuh" (stress on second syllable) | Pawt Augustuh | Named after Princess Augusta of Saxe-Gotha; locals say "pawt" not "port". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta,_South_Australia |
| Quorn | "kwork", "kwohrn", or "kworn" (rhyming with "born") | "KWORN" (rhymes with "born" but with a sharp K at the start) | Kworn | Named after Quorndon, Leicestershire. Locals say one syllable: KWORN. https://www.aussietowns.com.au/town/quorn-sa |
| Stirling North | "STUR-ling North" or "STIRL-ing" | "STURL-ing North" | Sturling North | Locals call it "Port Augusta South"; stress on first syllable, not second. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stirling_North |
| Wilmington | "WIL-ming-ton" (three distinct syllables) | "WILL-ming-tun" (two syllables, unstressed final syllable) | Willmingtun | Originally called "Beautiful Valley". Locals compress to two syllables. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_South_Australia |
| Woolundunga | "wool-un-DUNG-ah" or "wool-un-DOONG-ah" | "wool-UN-dung-uh" (stress on second syllable) | Woolundunga | Aboriginal: springs near Mount Brown. TTS stresses wrong syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolundunga,_South_Australia |
| Booleroo Centre | "bool-AIR-oo" or "bool-ER-oo Centre" | "bool-UH-roo Centre" (stress on first syllable) | Booleroo Centre | Aboriginal: possibly "plenty" or "soft mud". TTS over-emphasises the second syllable. https://ourlanguages.org.au/the-a-z-of-the-meanings-of-south-australias-town-names/ |
| Iron Knob | "EYE-rn Nob" or "eye-URN knob" | "EYE-urn Nob" (two syllables, not one) | Iron Nob | Named for the iron ore outcrop; TTS often compresses to one syllable. https://ourlanguages.org.au/the-a-z-of-the-meanings-of-south-australias-town-names/ |
| Hawker | "HACK-er" or "HAWK-er" (rhyming with "stalker") | "HAW-ker" (rhymes with "talker") | Hawker | Named after G.C. Hawker, SA politician. Stress on first syllable. https://ourlanguages.org.au/the-a-z-of-the-meanings-of-south-australias-town-names/ |
| Kanyaka | "KAN-ya-ka" (three even syllables) | "KAN-ya-kuh" (final 'a' is a schwa, almost silent) | Kan-ya-kuh | Aboriginal: locals say the second 'a' is effectively silent. From Kanyaka Station ruins, 42 km north of Quorn. https://www.aussietowns.com.au/town/quorn-sa |
| Cradock | "KRAY-dock" | "KRAD-ock" (short 'a' as in "cat") | Kradock | Small settlement 74 km NE of Quorn; named after Cradock in England. Locals use short 'a'. https://www.aussietowns.com.au/town/quorn-sa |
| Yourambulla | "yoo-RAM-bull-ah" or "your-AM-bull-ah" | "yoo-RAM-bull-uh" (stress on second syllable) | Yurambulla | Aboriginal art site near Hawker; important cave paintings. TTS reverses stress. https://www.aussietowns.com.au/town/quorn-sa |
| Wilpena | "will-PEN-ah" | "will-PEE-nuh" (long 'e' in second syllable) | Willpeena | Aboriginal: the great basin/pound in the Flinders Ranges. TTS shortens the vowel. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilpena_Pound |

## Roads, freeways & highways

| Name | Avoid | Correct | Alias respelling | Notes & source |

| Augusta Highway | "aw-GUS-ta" | "aw-GUS-tuh" (schwa ending) | Augustuh Highway | Named after Port Augusta. Locals pronounce the final 'a' as a schwa. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Highway |
| Stuart Highway | "STOO-art" or "STYOO-art" | "STOO-urt" (two syllables, short second vowel) | Stoo-urt Highway | Named after explorer John McDouall Stuart. Stress on first syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Highway |
| Eyre Highway | "EYE-ruh" or "AIR" | "EYE-uh" (two syllables, silent 'r') | Eye-uh Highway | Named after explorer Edward John Eyre. TTS often adds a hard 'r'. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyre_Highway |
| Horrocks Highway | "HOR-rocks" | "HOR-ruks" (short 'u' in second syllable) | Horrocks Highway | Named after explorer John Horrocks. Locals use short 'u' not 'o' in second syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horrocks_Highway |
| Pichi Richi Pass | "PITCH-ee RITCH-ee" or "pich-EYE rich-EYE" | "PICH-ee RICH-ee" (stress on first syllable of each word) | Pitchy Richy Pass | Aboriginal origin: believed to mean "narrow" or describe the pass. TTS reverses stress. https://www.aussietowns.com.au/town/quorn-sa |
