# Queenstown (7XS 92.1) Pronunciation Guide

Locally-correct pronunciations for West Coast Tasmania traffic reads on 7XS 92.1, with ElevenLabs v3 alias respellings. The West Coast's names are a mix of palawa (Aboriginal Tasmanian), Cornish-miner, Dutch-explorer, and British-colonial — the palawa and miner-era names are where TTS most often gets it wrong. Strahan is Tasmania's most famously mispronounced town. Each entry should be auditioned in the ElevenLabs studio before going to air.

## How to read this

Alias respellings only (v3 ignores phoneme/IPA); each replaces only the proper-noun token; audition before air.

## Suburbs & localities

| Strahan | "STRAN" or "STRAY-han" (spelling guess) | STRAWN (one syllable, rhymes with "prawn") | Strawn | CRITICAL. Tasmania's most famous mispronounced town. Locals say it as one syllable rhyming with "prawn", "lawn", or "yawn". Wikipedia confirms IPA /ˈstrɔːn/. Discover Tasmania local guide: "don't try to sound out the 'aha!' — one syllable is all you need." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strahan,_Tasmania |
| Zeehan | "ZEE-han" (aspirated H) or "zee-HAN" (wrong stress) | ZEE-an (two syllables, H is silent) | Zee-an | Named after Abel Tasman's ship "Zeehaen" (Dutch). The H is silent in local usage. Wikipedia confirms IPA /ˈziːən/. West Coast mining town, "Silver City". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeehan |
| Tullah | "TULL-uh" (schwa ending) or "TOO-lah" (wrong vowel) | TULL-ah (short 'u' as in "put", stress on first syllable) | Tull-ah | Palawa-derived name. West Coast mining town on Lake Rosebery. Short 'u' vowel, not long 'oo'. Two syllables. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullah |
| Rosebery | "ROZE-ber-ee" (four syllables, over-enunciated) | ROZ-bree (two syllables, compressed) | Roz-bree | West Coast mining town. Locals compress to two syllables: "ROZ-bree", not the full four-syllable spelling. Named after Lord Rosebery. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosebery,_Tasmania |
| Gormanston | "gor-MAN-ston" (wrong stress) | GOR-mans-ton (stress on first syllable, three syllables) | Gor-mans-ton | Named after Viscount Gormanston, Governor of Tasmania 1893–1900. Ghost town on the slopes of Mount Owen above Queenstown. Stress firmly on first syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gormanston,_Tasmania |
| Corinna | "KOR-in-ah" (wrong stress) | ko-RIN-ah (stress on second syllable) | Ko-rin-ah | Wilderness village on the Pieman River, gateway to the Tarkine. Named after a miner's wife. Stress on second syllable. https://www.corinna.com.au/a-colourful-past |
| Teepookana | "TEE-poo-KAH-na" (wrong stress) | tee-POO-ka-na (stress on second syllable) | Tee-poo-ka-na | Palawa name for the historic port on Macquarie Harbour, 9 km from Strahan. Gold was shipped out through here in the 1880s. Four syllables, stress on "poo". https://eprints.utas.edu.au/22682/2/dennison-A-K-opt.pdf |
| Waratah | "war-AT-ah" (wrong stress) | WOR-uh-tah (three syllables, short 'o' as in "worry") | Wor-uh-tah | Palawa-derived name (waratah flower). Small mining town on the Murchison Highway north of Rosebery. Stress on first syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waratah,_Tasmania |
| Pillinger | "pil-IN-jer" (wrong stress) | PILL-in-jer (stress on first syllable) | Pill-in-jer | Named after TB Pillinger, government geologist. Historic mining settlement on the east shore of Macquarie Harbour. Stress on first syllable. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillinger,_Tasmania |
| Renison Bell | "ren-IH-son" (wrong stress on Renison) | REN-ih-son Bell (stress on first syllable of Renison) | Ren-ih-son | Named after prospector George Renison Bell. Zinc mining locality near Zeehan. Stress on first syllable of "Renison"; "Bell" is standard English. https://www.utas.edu.au/tasmanian-companion/biogs/E000835b.htm |

## Roads, freeways & highways

| Name | Avoid | Correct | Alias respelling | Notes & source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lyell Highway | "lie-ELL Highway" (two syllables) | LYL Highway (one syllable, rhymes with "isle" minus the Z) | Lyl | Named after Mount Lyell (Sir Charles Lyell). One syllable — TTS will almost always default to two ("lie-ELL"). The alias "Lyl" also covers Mount Lyell and the Lyell River. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyell_Highway |
| Murchison Highway | "mur-CHEE-son Highway" (soft CH) | MUR-kih-shun Highway (hard K, not soft CH as in "church") | Mur-kih-shun | Named after Mount Murchison (Sir Roderick Murchison). The "ch" is a hard K sound, not the "ch" in "church". Major route from Somerset (near Burnie) to Zeehan. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murchison_Highway |

## Bridges, tunnels & landmarks

| Name | Avoid | Correct | Alias respelling | Notes & source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Macquarie Harbour | "ma-QUAR-ee Harbour" (wrong vowel in second syllable) | ma-KWOR-ee Harbour (stress on second syllable, "kwor" not "quar") | Ma-kwor-ee | Named after Governor Lachlan Macquarie. Australia's second-largest natural harbour. The "qu" makes a "kw" sound; locals say "ma-KWOR-ee". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macquarie_Harbor |
| Heemskirk | "heem-SKIRK" (wrong stress) or "HEM-skirk" (wrong vowel) | HEEMS-kirk (stress on first syllable, long 'ee', aspirated H) | Heems-kirk | Dutch origin — named after the ship Heemskerk (Abel Tasman's 1642 expedition). Mount Heemskirk and Heemskirk River on the West Coast. The Dutch H is fully aspirated, and the vowel is a long "ee". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Heemskirk |
| Tarkine | "TAR-kin" (wrong vowel) or "tar-KEEN" (wrong stress) | TAR-keen (two syllables, stress on first, long 'ee') | Tar-keen | Palawa name (takayna) for the vast rainforest region between the Arthur and Pieman Rivers. In palawa: "tah-kye-nah". The anglicised form "Tarkine" is two syllables with stress on the first. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarkine |
| Cape Sorell | "SO-rel" (stress on first syllable) | so-REL (stress on second syllable) | So-rel | Headland guarding the entrance to Macquarie Harbour. Named after Rear Admiral John Sorell. Stress on second syllable; TTS will default to first. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Sorell |
